# Microscope Lab refinement

Two entry choices: guided comparison and free exploration. The guided path compares Paramecium cilia with flagella on a simplified E. coli model. Opening both feature descriptions unlocks a reflection question; it does not certify learning. Incorrect interpretations receive evidence-focused feedback. Completion connects observation to microbiology and invites another specimen or a return home.

Four locally rendered SVG teaching diagrams replace the old generated-photo presentation: Paramecium, E. coli, Penicillium and influenza. Added colors, exaggerated structures, transparency and non-comparable sizes are explicitly labeled. Digital enlargement is not claimed as optical magnification. Influenza's electron-microscopy distinction is stated. No face or mushroom-cap overlay is presented as specimen morphology. Diagrams need further editorial scientific illustration review before release.

The new route component no longer consumes the legacy viewport, fact carousel, equipment scene or their content. Those files are retained for recovery but are not rendered by the lab. The new lab has no score writes, persistence or automatic achievement claims. Observations are session-only and this is stated. The existing route access/schedule gate remains unchanged.

- OpenStax, Groups of Protists: https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/23-3-groups-of-protists
- OpenStax, Fungi: https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/5-3-fungi

No organism is identified or diagnosed from an actual specimen. There is no physical sample handling activity.

## Real-image revision

Real licensed micrographs now lead the experience, with optional diagram help. See public/assets/micrographs/LICENSE.md for image provenance, rights, preparation limits, and scale notes. Selected visible features get synchronized image-region outlines and accessible numbered controls; features not reliably resolved are marked diagram-only and do not receive invented image annotations. Diagram help also marks the selected structure.

Every specimen now has a short introduction: what type of microbe it is, where it is found, why it matters (optional), and an observation mission. Additional factual sources: https://www.cdc.gov/ecoli/about/index.html ; https://www.cdc.gov/flu/about/disease/index.htm ; OpenStax protist and fungus chapters listed above. Image-region annotations remain teaching aids requiring specialist editorial review, not validated scientific segmentation.
